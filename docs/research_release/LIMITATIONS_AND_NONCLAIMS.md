# Limitations and Scientific Non-Claims: Canary SSM V3
## Hierarchical, Inertia-Constrained State-Space Models for Safer Software Rollout Decisions

- **Research Integrity Standard:** Gate G4 (Novelty & Non-Claim Boundaries)
- **Governing Protocol:** `docs/ANTIGRAVITY_SPEC_V3.md` & `docs/PREREGISTRATION_V3.md`

---

### 1. Mandatory Scientific Non-Claims

In accordance with academic standards and preregistered guidelines, the following boundaries are formally established:

#### 1.1 No Claim of a Novel Mathematical Model Family
We do **not** claim to have invented a new class of state-space models. The underlying mathematical filter is a standard linear-Gaussian Kalman filter / Dynamic Linear Model (DLM) with orthonormal sum-to-zero class deviations and Joseph-form covariance updates. The scientific contribution lies in the **methodological integration**, **principled finite-sample order-statistic threshold calibration (Gate G2)**, and **rigorous multi-detector benchmarking under software rollout confounders**.

#### 1.2 No Claim of Production Deployment Readiness
The Canary SSM V3 pipeline is an **academic research prototype and simulation benchmark**. It has not undergone shadow-mode production validation on live industrial cloud fleets. It is not approved for unmonitored automated deployment rollbacks in mission-critical environments.

#### 1.3 No Claim of Multivariate Telemetry Validation
All empirical evaluations in this research archive are strictly **univariate** (tracking request latency as the primary service health metric). While the state-space framework naturally admits multivariate extensions (e.g., joint latency, error rate, and saturation modeling), multi-metric covariance estimation and cross-metric threshold calibration were not evaluated and are deferred to future research.

#### 1.4 No Claim of Causal Attribution
State-space estimates $\hat{a}_{t|t}$ represent statistical projections conditioned on pre-period nuisance estimates ($\mu_i, \beta_i, \lambda_i$) and fixed process noise priors ($\mathbf{Q}$). They do not constitute formal causal counterfactuals or causal attribution proofs.

### 2. Methodological & Empirical Limitations

#### 2.1 The Hierarchical Shrinkage Trade-Off (Localized Fault Suppression)
A fundamental property of hierarchical shrinkage is that localized signals on individual hosts are shrunk toward the fleet-wide mean. 
- When an anomaly degrades all or several canary instances, hierarchical pooling speeds up detection by $-2.53$ to $-5.39\text{ min}$.
- **The Cost:** When a defect is confined strictly to a single canary instance (`single_host_persistent`), hierarchical pooling delays detection by **+9.46 minutes** ($30.89\text{ min}$ vs $21.43\text{ min}$ for unpooled SSM). For single-instance failure modes (e.g., bad local cache, memory leak on one node), unpooled detectors or extreme-value detectors (`topk_cusum`) are superior.

#### 2.2 Lack of Benefit from Two-Scale Class Inertia (H2 Rejection)
Hypothesis H2 preregistered that modeling class-level autoregressive dynamics ($\boldsymbol{\delta}_t$) would improve detection over fleet-level filtering. The empirical results demonstrated conclusively that class-level inertia provided **zero practical benefit** ($\Delta = -0.18\text{ min}$, $p = 0.42$; all confidence intervals within $\pm 1.0\text{ min}$). Fleet-level state tracking is sufficient.

#### 2.3 The Confirmation Depth Latency Penalty (H3 Rejection)
Increasing confirmation depth $m \in \{3, 5, 10, 15\}$ (requiring sustained posterior evidence over $m$ consecutive minutes) effectively suppresses false alarms on isolated transient spikes. However, on subtle or slowly ramped degradations, it imposes an unavoidable detection delay penalty exceeding 3 minutes ($+4.12\text{ min}$ on `weak_persistent_3host` at $m=3$).

#### 2.4 Classical Baseline Competitiveness (H5 Rejection)
The proposed state-space model does **not** unilaterally dominate classical baselines across all operating regimes:
- Under clean, unconfounded step jumps or localized faults, windowed GLR (`glr_window`) and CUSUM (`adj_cusum`) detect anomalies rapidly with an order of magnitude lower computational overhead ($0.01\text{ ms}$ vs $0.42\text{ ms}$).
- The distinct advantage of the state-space formulation emerges specifically under **environmental confounders** (correlated class shocks, 20% missing telemetry, and heavy-tailed noise $t(3)$), where classical baselines degrade severely.

### 3. Simulation & Environmental Limitations

#### 3.1 Synthetic Benchmark Data
All evaluations were conducted on synthetic telemetry generated by `canary_ssm/simulate.py`. While the simulator incorporates realistic diurnal workloads, correlated shocks, heavy-tailed noise, and missingness, synthetic data cannot replicate the full complexity, non-stationarity, and emergent failure modes of real cloud infrastructure.

#### 3.2 Host Count Scaling
The benchmark evaluated fleets of $N = 100$ hosts (10 canary instances). Scaling behavior to hyper-scale fleets ($N \ge 10,000$ hosts) requires hierarchical sub-clustering or distributed Kalman filtering to avoid large observation covariance matrices.

#### 3.3 Platform-Specific Floating-Point Reproducibility
As documented in `docs/final_audit/TEST_SUITE_AUDIT.md`, 1-ULP floating-point differences between Windows MSVC UCRT and Linux glibc prevent bit-identical SHA-256 hash matching on raw floating-point simulator outputs (`test_legacy_simulator_outputs_are_bit_identical_after_v3_edits`). Numerical distributions, means, variances, and statistical tests reproduce identically across platforms.
